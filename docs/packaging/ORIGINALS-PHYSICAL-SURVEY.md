## 0. How to read this annex

Every fact carries a provenance tag:

- **[READ: source]** -- read by me in the named scan, record or page during this task.
- **[MEAS: method]** -- measured by me from a scan (method stated). Scans are often cropped
  or padded, so measured trim sizes are good to about +/- 3 mm unless stated otherwise.
- **[INF]** -- my inference from what I read or saw; plausible, not confirmed.
- **[MEM]** -- recalled from general knowledge, not verified in a source during this task.

Typeface identifications are visual (no font metadata exists in a scan) and are therefore
**[INF]** even when I am fairly sure. The scans are in `initech-manuals-ref/<product>/`; file
names below are relative to that directory.

## 7. Cross-cutting observations (what the originals have in common)

1. **Trim.** The application manuals cluster at about 7 x 9 in (Lotus, Borland, Ashton-Tate,
   Apple). IBM used 5.5 x 8.5 in half-size binders; Microsoft about 6.5 x 9; WordPerfect about
   8 x 9 **[MEAS/INF, as above]**.
2. **Binding follows document role.** Reference material that is consulted and updated lives
   in ring binders in slipcases (IBM, Ashton-Tate, WordPerfect). Tutorials, workbooks and
   language manuals are perfect-bound (Lotus, Borland, WordPerfect Workbook, Apple) **[READ/MEM]**.
3. **Colour.** Interiors are black only (IBM, Borland, Lotus) or black plus one colour
   (Ashton-Tate, WordPerfect: blue) **[READ]**. Colour is spent on covers, tabs and dividers.
4. **Folios.** Chapter-page numbering ("4-26", "UI-3", "6-3") is the norm for reference
   manuals; Borland and Apple number continuously **[READ]**.
5. **Hanging side heads** (heads in a narrow left column, text in a wider right column) are
   used by Ashton-Tate, WordPerfect, IBM and Borland **[READ]**.
6. **Every box carries the same paper kit:** license (sealed envelope or statement), warranty
   registration card, quick reference, keyboard template(s), support guide, product flyers,
   and often an order form or media coupon **[READ: dBASE, Lotus, Borland, WordPerfect lists]**.
7. **Part and print codes are everywhere** (IBM 7-character part numbers and edition notices;
   Ashton-Tate 5+4 digit numbers and month/year codes; Borland "11MN-PAS02-60 BOR 1850" plus a
   printer's key; WordPerfect "MNUS..." codes; Apple "Z030-xxxx-A") **[READ]**.
8. **One company, one line look.** IBM is the only one of these that ran a rigorous
   cross-product package system (common slipcase, colour-coded family bands, fixed logo and
   copy positions) **[READ: four IBM packages photographed]**. It is the best model for a bland
   corporate line.
