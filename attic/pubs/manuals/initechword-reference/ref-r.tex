\begin{Feature}{Replace}
Replace finds text or codes, as Search does, and puts other text in their
place.  It can replace every occurrence at once, or stop at each one and ask
you.

To replace,
\begin{WPSteps}
\item Move the cursor to where the replacing is to begin: Replace works from
the cursor to the end of the document.  (To go to the top of the document,
press \K{Home, Home, Up}.)
\item Press \FK{Replace}{Alt-F2}.  The status line asks
\Scr{w/Confirm? No (Yes)}.
\item Type \Typed{y} to be asked at each occurrence
\Or
Type \Typed{n} or press \K{Enter} to replace every occurrence without
asking.
\item At \Scr{-> Srch:}, type the text to be replaced and press \K{F2}.
\item At \Scr{Replace with:}, type the new text and press \K{F2}.
\end{WPSteps}
\Documents{wpkey}{Alt-F2}
\Documents{wpkey}{Rplc:F2}
\Documents{wpkey}{Rplc:Esc}
InitechWord replaces the text wherever it finds it, from the cursor to the
end of the document, and leaves the cursor after the last replacement.  When
there is nothing more to find, the status line shows \Scr{* Not found *};
press any key to go on.

\BeginReplay{ref-replace}
For example, type
\Type{The memo of Friday replaces the memo of Monday.  File the MEMO.}
press \PressKey{Home, Home, Up} and \Press{Replace}{Alt-F2}, type
\TypeName{n}, type \TypeName{memo} and press \PressKey{F2}:
\WordScreen[rows=1]{ref-replace-1}{The replacement prompt}
Type \TypeName{notice} and press \PressKey{F2}:
\WordScreen[rows=2]{ref-replace-2}{Every memo replaced}
Each \textit{memo} is now \textit{notice}, and the capitals of \textit{MEMO}
are kept: \textit{NOTICE}.
\EndReplay

\Notes
\Topic{Canceling}
Press \FK{Cancel}{F1} at \Scr{w/Confirm? No (Yes)}, at the search string or
at \Scr{Replace with:} to leave Replace without replacing anything.  At
\Scr{Replace? Yes (No)}, Cancel stops the replacing; what was replaced
before it stays replaced.
\Documents{wpkey}{Rplc:F1}

\Topic{Capital Letters}
The search string finds capitals as described under \InRef{Search}.  When
the new text is all in lower case, InitechWord gives it the capitals of the
text it replaces: replacing \Typed{memo} with \Typed{notice} gives
\Scr{notice} for \Scr{memo}, \Scr{Notice} for \Scr{Memo} and \Scr{NOTICE}
for \Scr{MEMO}.  New text with any capital is put in exactly as you typed
it.

\Topic{Codes}
The search string can hold the codes Search can find (see \InRef{Search}).
Replacing \Code{[BOLD]} with nothing, for example, deletes every bold on
code; the \Code{[bold]} codes stay where they are, as Reveal Codes shows,
and change nothing.  To delete them too, replace \Code{[bold]} with
nothing: at the \Scr{-> Srch:} prompt press F6 twice, which gives
\Code{[BOLD][bold]}, then \K{Left} twice and \K{Delete}, which leaves
\Code{[bold]}.  The new text can hold \Code{[Tab]}
(\K{Tab}), \Code{[\rarr Indent]} (\K{F4}), \Code{[HRt]} (\K{Enter}) and
\Code{[HPg]} (\K{Ctrl-Enter}), but no appearance codes: F6 and F8 do nothing
at \Scr{Replace with:}.
\Documents{wpkey}{Rplc:Tab}
\Documents{wpkey}{Rplc:F4}
\Documents{wpkey}{Rplc:Enter}
\Documents{wpkey}{Rplc:Ctrl-Enter}

\Topic{Confirming Each Replacement}
If you answered \Typed{y} to \Scr{w/Confirm? No (Yes)}, InitechWord stops
at each occurrence, highlights it as a block and asks
\Scr{Replace? Yes (No)}.  Type \Typed{y} or press \K{Enter} to replace it, or
type \Typed{n} to leave it; InitechWord then goes on to the next.  Other
keys are ignored.

\BeginReplay{ref-replace-confirm}
For example, type
\Type{Send the report to Bill.  The report is due today.  Report early.}
press \PressKey{Home, Home, Up} and \Press{Replace}{Alt-F2}, type
\TypeName{y}, type \TypeName{report}, press \PressKey{F2}, type
\TypeName{memo} and press \PressKey{F2}:
\WordScreen[rows=1]{ref-replace-confirm-1}{Replace asks at each occurrence}
Type \TypeName{y} for the first, \TypeName{n} for the second and
\TypeName{y} for the third:
\WordScreen[rows=1]{ref-replace-confirm-2}{The first and third replaced}
\EndReplay

\Topic{Deleting Text Throughout}
To delete every occurrence of a word, replace it with nothing: at
\Scr{Replace with:} press F2 without typing anything.

\Topic{Direction}
Replace works forward, from the cursor to the end of the document.  To
replace backward, from the cursor to the beginning, press \K{Up} at the
\Scr{-> Srch:} prompt: it becomes \Scr{<- Srch:}.  \K{Down} turns it forward
again.

\Topic{Editing the Strings}
The search string and the new text are typed and corrected with the keys of
the \Scr{Srch:} prompt (see \InRef{Search}): \K{Left}, \K{Right},
\K{Backspace} and \K{Delete}, a whole code at a time.  \K{F2} or \K{Esc}
ends each string.  At \Scr{Replace with:} InitechWord does not look at
Shift, Alt or Ctrl on these keys:
\Documents{wpkey}{Rplc:Left}
\Documents{wpkey}{Rplc:Right}
\Documents{wpkey}{Rplc:Backspace}
\Documents{wpkey}{Rplc:Delete}

\Topic{Repeating}
Replace starts with an empty search string.  Afterwards, the search string
of Replace is offered by the next Search (F2), so that you can look for any
occurrence you chose not to replace.

\Topic{Undoing a Replacement}
Replace does not keep what it replaced.  To go back, retrieve the document
as you last saved it, or replace the new text with the old.

\SeeAlso{Block; Codes; Search; Undelete}
\end{Feature}

\begin{Feature}{Retrieve}
Retrieve brings a document you have saved back onto the screen, so that you
can read it or change it.  You can also
retrieve a document into the one on the screen, to combine the two.

To retrieve a document,
\begin{WPSteps}
\item Clear the screen if it holds a document you do not want to combine
with the one you retrieve (see \InRef{Exit}).
\item Press \FK{Retrieve}{Shift-F10}.  The status line shows
\Scr{Document to be Retrieved:}.
\PullDown{Select \Mi{R}etrieve from the \Mi{F}ile menu.}
\item Type the name of the document and press \K{Enter}.
\end{WPSteps}
\Documents{wpkey}{Shift-F10}
On a clear screen the document you retrieve becomes the document you are
working on: its name is shown on the status line and the cursor is at its
top.

\Notes
\Topic{Into Another Document}
If the screen already holds text, the retrieved document's text is put into
it at the cursor, and the document on the screen keeps its own name.  The
two documents are now one; the file you retrieved is not changed.

\BeginReplay{ref-retrieve}
For example, with a new document on the screen, type
\TypeRun{Attached is the letter: } (with the space), press
\Press{Retrieve}{Shift-F10}, type \TypeName{letter} -- the letter saved in
\textit{A Brief Lesson} -- and press \PressKey{Enter}:
\WordScreen[rows=6]{ref-retrieve}{A document retrieved into another, at the cursor}
\EndReplay
The text of the letter follows the text you typed, and the status line shows
no name: the document on the screen has not been saved.

\Topic{Messages}
\Scr{ERROR: File Not Found} means there is no document by that name on the
data volume: check the name and type it again.
\Scr{ERROR: Incompatible File Format} means the file is not a WordPerfect
5.1 document that InitechWord can read.
\Scr{ERROR: Document too large} means the file is larger than InitechWord
can hold (see Appendix H).  Press any key to return to the prompt.

\Topic{WordPerfect 5.1 Documents}
InitechWord's documents are WordPerfect 5.1 documents, and InitechWord
retrieves documents written by WordPerfect 5.1 itself, keeping every code
it holds (see \InRef{Codes}).  A WordPerfect document that holds a code
InitechWord does not know is refused with \Scr{ERROR: Incompatible File
Format}, rather than changed.

\SeeAlso{Exit; Filenames; Save}
\end{Feature}

\begin{Feature}{Return, Deletable Soft}
A deletable soft return ends a line without ending the paragraph.
InitechWord puts one in when you set the justification with the cursor in
the middle of a line, so that the text after the cursor starts a new line
in the new justification (see \InRef{Justification}).

Reveal Codes shows it as \Code{[DSRt]}.  To join the two lines again, delete
the code: InitechWord deletes it without asking.

\SeeAlso{Justification; Return, Hard; Return, Soft}
\end{Feature}

\begin{Feature}{Return, Hard}
A hard return ends a line where you choose, and ends the paragraph.  You put
one in by pressing \K{Enter}.  Use it at the end of a paragraph, after each
line of an address or a list, and to make blank lines.

To insert a hard return,
\begin{WPSteps}
\item Press \K{Enter}.
\end{WPSteps}
\Documents{wpkey}{Enter}

\Notes
\Topic{Code}
A hard return is the code \Code{[HRt]}.  Delete it to join a line to the
line after it (see \InRef{Delete}).

\Topic{Shift-Enter and Alt-Enter}
Shift-Enter and Alt-Enter put in a hard return as Enter does.  Ctrl-Enter
puts in a hard page (see \InRef{Page Break, Hard}).

\SeeAlso{Page Break, Hard; Return, Soft; Word Wrap}
\end{Feature}

\begin{Feature}{Return, Soft}
A soft return is the end of a line that InitechWord makes for you when a
line is full (see \InRef{Word Wrap}).  It takes the place of the space
between the last word that fits on the line and the word that goes to the
next line.  Reveal Codes shows it as \Code{[SRt]}.

You never type a soft return and you need not delete one: whenever you
change a paragraph, InitechWord wraps it again and moves its soft returns to
where the lines now end.

\SeeAlso{Return, Deletable Soft; Return, Hard; Word Wrap}
\end{Feature}

\begin{Feature}{Reveal Codes}
Reveal Codes shows the hidden codes of the document.  The screen divides:
the upper part shows the document as usual, and the lower part shows the
same lines with every code in square brackets.  Use it to see why text
looks as it does, and to delete a code.

To reveal codes,
\begin{WPSteps}
\item Press \FK{Reveal Codes}{Alt-F3}.
\item Move the cursor as usual.  It moves in both parts of the screen; in
the codes area it is the highlighted character or code.
\item Press \FK{Reveal Codes}{Alt-F3} again to return to the full editing
screen.
\end{WPSteps}
\Documents{wpkey}{Alt-F3}
\Documents{wpkey}{F11}
\K{F11} does what Alt-F3 does, with Block on or off.
\FlairShot{reveal}{640}{440}{Reveal Codes: the bold codes around a word}

The Reveal Codes screen has five parts, from the top:
\begin{description}
\item[The document] ten lines of the document, as on the editing screen.
\item[The status line] as on the editing screen.
\item[The ruler] the margins and tab stops in effect at the cursor.
\item[The codes area] ten rows: the text of the lines around the cursor,
starting three lines above it, with the codes shown.  A line longer than the
screen runs on into the next row.
\item[The message] \Scr{Press Reveal Codes to restore screen}.
\end{description}

\Notes
\Topic{Deleting Codes}
In Reveal Codes, Backspace and Delete delete codes without asking, as they
delete characters.  Deleting either code of an appearance or size deletes
its partner too.  See \InRef{Codes}.

\Topic{Editing in Reveal Codes}
Everything you can do on the editing screen you can do in Reveal Codes:
type, block, move, format.  Both parts of the screen change as you work.

\Topic{The Cursor in the Codes Area}
The highlight covers the character or the whole code the cursor is on.  At
the very end of the document it is one empty cell after the last code.

\Topic{The End of the Document}
\BeginReplay{ref-reveal-end}At the end of the document the highlight is one empty cell after the
last character or code.  For example, type \TypeRun{Total:}, press
\PressKey{Tab}, type \TypeRun{570} and press \Press{Reveal Codes}{Alt-F3}:
\WordScreen[rows=13]{ref-reveal-end}{The cursor at the end of the document, in Reveal Codes}
\EndReplay

\Topic{The Ruler}
The left margin is shown by \Scr{\{} and the right margin by \Scr{\}}; a
margin that does not fall on a tab stop is shown by \Scr{[} or \Scr{]}
instead.  The small triangles are the tab stops, every half inch from the
left margin.

\SeeAlso{Codes; Margins, Left and Right; Tab}
\end{Feature}
