\section{Protecting your work}
\SideHead{Save}A document is in memory until you save it.  Save it every
few minutes, and before you leave the computer.
\SideHead{Shut down}Always use \menuitem{Shut Down} before you turn the
computer off.  It saves the arrangement of the desktop and gives the open
application the chance to save your work.
\SideHead{Empty the Trash with care}Items in the Trash still take up space
on the disk; emptying the Trash frees it, and cannot be undone.
\SideHead{Keep copies}Copy your documents to another disk at the end of the
day, with \file{COPY} or \file{XCOPY} at the InitechDOS prompt
(Chapter~\ref{ch:dos}).

\section{Checking the data disk}
\index{CHKDSK}
From time to time, check the data disk with \file{CHKDSK}: leave FLAIR,
change to the \file{APPS} directory and type \type{CHKDSK} (Chapter~\ref{ch:dos}).
The \doctitle{InitechDOS Reference} explains its report and what to do if
it finds a problem.

\section{When the disk is nearly full}
A new folder cannot be made, and documents cannot be saved, when the data
disk is full.  Empty the Trash, then move documents you no longer need
onto another disk at the InitechDOS prompt.

\section{If the power fails}
\index{power failure}
If the power fails, or the computer is turned off while FLAIR is running,
everything in the computer's memory is lost: documents you had not saved,
the Clipboard, and any change to the arrangement of the desktop made since
FLAIR last recorded it.
\begin{TaskSteps}
\Step When the power returns, start the computer as usual.
\Result{FLAIR starts with the arrangement it last recorded.}
\Step Leave FLAIR with \menuitem{Exit to InitechDOS} and check the data disk
with \file{CHKDSK}.
\Result{If \file{CHKDSK} reports a problem, follow the
\doctitle{InitechDOS Reference}.}
\Step Restart the computer, open your documents, and check that the last
changes you saved are there.
\end{TaskSteps}

\section{Handling disks}
\index{disks!handling}
\begin{itemize}
\item Keep diskettes in their envelopes or a disk box when they are not in
  a drive, upright and out of the sun.
\item Do not bend a diskette, and do not touch the magnetic surface
  through the opening in its jacket.
\item Write on a diskette's label with a soft felt-tip pen, or write the
  label before you put it on the diskette.
\item Keep diskettes away from magnets, including those in telephones,
  loudspeakers and the display.
\item Never take a disk out of its drive while the drive's light is on, or
  while FLAIR is running.  Shut the computer down first.
\end{itemize}

\section{Cleaning the mouse}
\index{mouse!cleaning}
When the pointer moves in jerks, or not at all in one direction, clean the
mouse.
\begin{TaskSteps}
\Step Shut the computer down and turn it off.
\Step Turn the mouse over and turn the ring round the ball to free it.
\Step Take out the ball and wash it in warm water with a little soap.  Dry
it with a lint-free cloth.
\Step Clean the rollers inside the mouse with a cotton swab moistened with
water, and blow out any dust.
\Step Put the ball back and lock the ring.
\end{TaskSteps}

\section{Looking after the equipment}
\begin{itemize}
\item Keep the computer, the display and the disks away from heat, damp
  and dust, and leave room round the computer for air to flow.
\item Keep disks away from magnets, telephones and the display, and do not
  touch their surfaces.
\item Clean the display with a soft cloth; never spray liquid on it.
\item Turn the computer off before you connect or disconnect the keyboard,
  the mouse or the display.
\item Clean the mouse ball and rollers when the pointer stops moving
  smoothly (see ``Cleaning the mouse,'' earlier in this chapter).
\item Do not eat or drink at the keyboard.  If you spill something on it,
  turn the computer off at once and have the keyboard cleaned before you
  use it again.
\item Before you move the computer, shut it down, turn it off, and take
  the disks out of their drives.
\end{itemize}
