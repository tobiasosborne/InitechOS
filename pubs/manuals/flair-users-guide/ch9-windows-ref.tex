\section{Kinds of windows}
\index{windows!kinds of}
\SideHead{Document windows}Finder windows and the windows of most
applications.  They have every part described below.
Figure~\ref{fig:rootwin} shows a Finder window and Figure~\ref{fig:dtyped}
an InitechText window.

\SideHead{Accessory windows}The Calculator's window is smaller, with
rounded corners, a dark title bar and only a close box.  It can be moved
by its title bar.

\SideHead{Alert boxes}A box with a message and one or more buttons, which
FLAIR or an application shows when it needs your answer before it goes
on.  An alert box has no title bar and cannot be moved.  While it is on the
screen nothing else responds: click one of its buttons, or press a key
that chooses one.  Figure~\ref{fig:emptyask} is an example.

\SideHead{Dialog boxes}A box in which you give an application information,
such as the name of a document (Chapter~\ref{ch:appsref}).  Like an alert, it must be
answered before you go on.  Figure~\ref{fig:textsaveas} is an example.

\SideHead{Progress boxes}A box with a title bar and a progress bar, which
an application shows while it works, such as InitechCalc's
\texttt{FILE COPY} box.  It goes away by itself when the work is done; until
then, clicks and keys are ignored.  The bar fills from left to right as the
work goes on.

\section{How windows behave}
\SideHead{The active window}One window is active at a time: the front
window.  A click in another window makes it active and brings it to the
front; that first click does nothing else.  When an application's window
becomes active, the lower menu bar changes to that application's menus.

\SideHead{Boxes}A box acts when you release the mouse button inside it.
Press the button in the box and move out before releasing it to change your
mind.

\SideHead{Moving}While you drag a title bar, an outline of the window
follows the pointer; the window moves when you release the button.  A
window cannot be moved under the menu bars.

\SideHead{Size}While you drag the size box, an outline shows the new size.
A window is at least 96 dots wide and 64 dots high, and no larger than the
screen.

\SideHead{Zooming}A zoomed window fills the screen below the menu bars,
with a small margin.  Zooming again returns it to the size and place it
had.  Changing the size of a zoomed window ends the zoomed state.

\SideHead{Collapsing}A collapsed window keeps only its title bar.  It can
be moved and closed, and a click in its collapse box rolls it down again.

\SideHead{Scrolling}An arrow moves the contents a little, and keeps
moving them while you hold down the button.  A click in the grey area
above or below the scroll box moves them by a windowful.  The scroll box
shows where the part you see lies in the whole, and can be dragged.
Scroll bars act only in the active window.

\SideHead{Buttons}In an alert or dialog box, the button with a heavy
border is the \term{default button}: pressing \key{Enter} chooses it.  When
there is a \texttt{Cancel} button, pressing \key{Esc} chooses it.

\section{Window limits}
At most four Finder windows can be open at a time, and an application from
the disk may have its own windows besides.  When a window does not open,
close another window first.
