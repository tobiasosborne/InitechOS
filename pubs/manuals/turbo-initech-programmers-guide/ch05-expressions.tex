\chapter{Expressions}
\ix{expressions}

An \term{expression} computes a value from \term{operands} (constants,
variable references, function calls) and \term{operators}.  This chapter
gives the syntax of expressions, the precedence of the operators, the
operand types each operator accepts, and the rule of complete evaluation.

\ix{precedence of operators}
The syntax gives the operators four levels of precedence:

\begin{BorTable}{Precedence of operators}{p{40mm}p{20mm}p{38mm}}
\Hd{Operators} & \Hd{Level} & \Hd{Category}\EndHd
\code{-} (unary), \rw{not} & first (highest) & unary operators\\
\code{*}, \rw{div}, \rw{mod}, \rw{and} & second & multiplying operators\\
\code{+}, \code{-}, \rw{or} & third & adding operators\\
\code{= <> < <= > >=} & fourth (lowest) & relational operators\\
\end{BorTable}

An operand between two operators of different precedence belongs to the
operator of higher precedence; between operators of the same precedence it
belongs to the operator on its left.  Parentheses override these rules.
So \code{2 + 3 * 4} is 14, \code{10 - 4 - 3} is 3, and \code{(2 + 3) * 4}
is 20.

The unary minus and \rw{not} apply to the factor that follows them, so
\code{-2 * -3} is 6 and \code{not A or B} means \code{(not A) or B}.

\ix{relational operators, chained}
An expression contains at most one relational operator outside
parentheses.  \code{A < B = C} is an error; write \code{(A < B) = C}.
Because the relational operators have the lowest precedence, operands that
are themselves Boolean expressions must be parenthesized:
\code{(I > 0) and (I < 10)}.

\section{Arithmetic operators}
\ix{arithmetic operators}
\ix{div}
\ix{mod}
\begin{BorTable}{Arithmetic operators}{p{12mm}p{36mm}p{22mm}p{22mm}}
\Hd{Operator} & \Hd{Operation} & \Hd{Operands} & \Hd{Result}\EndHd
\code{+} & addition & \rw{integer} & \rw{integer}\\
\code{-} & subtraction & \rw{integer} & \rw{integer}\\
\code{*} & multiplication & \rw{integer} & \rw{integer}\\
\rw{div} & integer division & \rw{integer} & \rw{integer}\\
\rw{mod} & remainder & \rw{integer} & \rw{integer}\\
\code{-} (unary) & sign inversion & \rw{integer} & \rw{integer}\\
\end{BorTable}

\rw{div} divides and discards the fraction, rounding toward zero.
\rw{mod} gives the remainder of that division; its sign is the sign of the
left operand, so that \code{(A div B) * B + A mod B} equals \id{A}.
Division by zero is not defined; a program must not divide by zero.
All arithmetic is 32-bit: a result outside the range of \rw{integer}
wraps around (Chapter 3).  There is no division operator \code{/} and no
real arithmetic.

\subsection{Complete evaluation}
\ix{complete evaluation}
\ix{short-circuit evaluation}
Turbo Initech always evaluates both operands of \rw{and} and
\rw{or}, even when the left operand alone decides the result.  This is
\term{complete evaluation}.  It is a rule of the language, not an option:
every program behaves the same way whatever its operands are.

The first condition is \rw{false} as soon as \code{Check(false)} returns,
but \code{Check(true)} is still called; the counter shows two calls for each
condition.

\subsection{Writing guards}
\ix{guards}
\ix{nested-if guard}
Because both operands are evaluated, a test that protects another test
cannot be combined with it by \rw{and}.  In
\code{(I <= Size) and (Table[I] = Wanted)} the element \code{Table[I]} is
read even when \id{I} is outside the array.  Write such a guard as two
nested \rw{if} statements, so that the second test is reached only when the

\section{Relational operators}
\ix{relational operators}
\ix{comparison}
\begin{BorTable}{Relational operators}{p{12mm}p{36mm}p{48mm}}
\Hd{Operator} & \Hd{Operation} & \Hd{Operand types}\EndHd
\code{=} & equal & two of the same simple type, or strings\\
\code{<>} & not equal & the same\\
\code{<} & less than & the same\\
\code{<=} & less than or equal & the same\\
\code{>} & greater than & the same\\
\code{>=} & greater than or equal & the same\\
\end{BorTable}

Both operands must be of the same type: two integers, two Boolean values or
two characters.  The result is of type \rw{boolean}.  Integers compare by
value, Boolean values with \code{false < true}, and characters by their
codes.  Records, arrays and files cannot be compared.

\section{String operators}
\ix{string operators}
\ix{concatenation}
When at least one operand of \code{+} is a string, \code{+} joins the two
operands into one string (\term{concatenation}); the other operand may be a
string or a character.  The result is at most 255 characters long; the
part beyond the 255th character is lost.  Two characters cannot be joined
with \code{+}; one of the operands must be a string.

When at least one operand of a relational operator is a string, the
operands are compared as strings; the other operand may be a string or a
character.  Strings are compared character by character, by the codes of
the characters; when one string is the beginning of the other, the shorter
string is the smaller.  So \code{'Pete' < 'Peter'} and
\code{'Peter' < 'Gibbons'} is \rw{false}.

\section{Function calls}
\ix{function calls}
A factor may call a function: the function name followed by its arguments
in parentheses.  The parentheses are required even when the function has
no parameters, as in \code{Count()}.  Chapter 7 describes the rules for
arguments.  The standard functions \std{ord}{Ord}, \std{chr}{Chr} and
\std{length}{Length} are written in the same form (Chapter 9).

\section{Operand types}
\ix{type compatibility}
Turbo Initech converts no value from one type to another by itself, with
one exception: a character used with a string in \code{+} or a relation
is treated as a string of one character.  Every other change of type is
written with \std{ord}{Ord} or \std{chr}{Chr}.  In particular, a Boolean or
character value is never an integer, and an integer is never a condition.
